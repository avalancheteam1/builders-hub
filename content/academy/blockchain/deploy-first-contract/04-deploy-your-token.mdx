This is the moment. Three fields, one button, and your contract is on-chain.

## What You Are Deploying

An ERC-20 token. "ERC-20" is just an agreed list of functions that a token contract should have — `transfer`, `balanceOf`, `approve`, and a few more. Because every token follows the same list, every wallet and exchange knows how to talk to any of them without special handling.

Here is the part of the contract that your three answers control:

```solidity
contract WorkshopToken {
    string public name;
    string public symbol;
    uint8 public constant decimals = 18;

    uint256 public totalSupply;

    mapping(address => uint256) public balanceOf;

    constructor(string memory name_, string memory symbol_, uint256 initialSupply) {
        name = name_;
        symbol = symbol_;

        totalSupply = initialSupply * 10 ** decimals;
        balanceOf[msg.sender] = totalSupply;

        emit Transfer(address(0), msg.sender, totalSupply);
    }
}
```

A few things worth noticing:

- **The `constructor` runs exactly once**, at deployment, and never again. Whatever you type into the form is passed in here and written permanently into the contract.
- **`msg.sender` is you.** The entire supply is credited to whoever deploys, so the tokens land in your wallet.
- **`mapping(address => uint256) public balanceOf`** is the whole ledger. A token is, at heart, a table of who owns how much.
- **`decimals = 18`** means the contract stores 1 token as `1000000000000000000`. Wallets divide by that before showing you a balance. You type whole tokens in the form and the constructor does the multiplication.

<Callout type="info">
This contract is written out in full rather than inheriting from a library, so you can read the entire token in one screen. For a token holding real value, use a reviewed implementation such as [OpenZeppelin's ERC20](https://wizard.openzeppelin.com/#erc20) instead.
</Callout>

## Deploy It

Fill in the three fields and press **Deploy Token**. Your wallet will pop up asking you to sign — that signature is what makes it yours.

<Callout type="warn">
Your wallet will show a fee estimate before you confirm. On Fuji this is test AVAX and costs nothing real, but reading the estimate before signing is a habit worth building now.
</Callout>

## What Happened When You Pressed Deploy

<Steps>
<Step>

### Your inputs were encoded

The name, symbol and supply were packed together with the contract's compiled bytecode into a single transaction.

</Step>
<Step>

### You signed it

Your wallet used your private key to sign the transaction. This proves the deployment came from your account, without revealing the key.

</Step>
<Step>

### The network executed it

Validators ran your constructor, wrote the resulting contract into their copy of the chain, and agreed on the outcome.

</Step>
<Step>

### You got an address

Your contract now lives at a permanent address. Anyone in the world can call its functions, and nobody can change its code — including you.

</Step>
</Steps>
