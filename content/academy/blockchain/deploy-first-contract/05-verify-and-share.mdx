Your contract is deployed. Now let's prove it exists independently of this page.

## Find It on the Explorer

A block explorer is a public website that reads the blockchain. It is not owned by us, and it has no special access — it sees exactly what any node sees.

Take the contract address from the previous step and paste it into the Fuji explorer:

```
https://testnet.snowtrace.io/address/<your-contract-address>
```

You should see:

- **Contract creation** — the transaction that deployed it, and your address as the creator
- **Token name and symbol** — the values you chose, read straight from the contract
- **Total supply** — your number, with eighteen decimal places after it
- **One holder** — you, with all of it

<Callout type="info">
The explorer had no idea your contract existed a minute ago. It shows up because it is genuinely on the network, discovered the same way anyone else would discover it.
</Callout>

## Add It to Your Wallet

Your token will not appear in Core automatically. Wallets cannot know about every token ever created, so you tell it where to look — using the contract address as the pointer.

<Steps>
<Step>

### Open the token list

In Core, go to the **Tokens** tab and choose **Manage**.

</Step>
<Step>

### Add a custom token

Click **+ Add Custom Token** and paste your contract address.

</Step>
<Step>

### Confirm the details

Core reads the name, symbol and decimals directly from your contract and fills them in. If those match what you typed, your contract is answering questions correctly.

</Step>
<Step>

### Add it

Your full supply now shows in your wallet balance.

</Step>
</Steps>

<Callout type="warn">
Make sure Core is still on Fuji. A token deployed on Fuji only exists on Fuji — switch to Mainnet and it will not be there.
</Callout>

## Try Sending Some

You have a working token, so use it. Send a few to the person next to you, or to a second account of your own.

The transfer goes through the `transfer` function you read on the previous page. Watch it appear on the explorer under your contract's **Transfers** tab within a couple of seconds.

## What You Actually Learned

Strip away the token and here is what you did:

- Created an account secured by a private key that never left your machine
- Connected to a public blockchain network and funded your account
- Deployed immutable code to that network, paying for the storage it uses
- Verified the result through a third party with no connection to this site
- Interacted with your deployed code by sending a transaction

That is the whole shape of smart contract development. Everything else — bigger contracts, better tooling, real value — builds on exactly these steps.
