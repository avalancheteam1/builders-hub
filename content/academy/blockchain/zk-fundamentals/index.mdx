Welcome to the **ZK Fundamentals** course! The [Encrypted ERC course](/academy/blockchain/encrypted-erc) covers what eERC does and why it exists. This course covers how it works underneath.

We'll start with why privacy is hard on a public ledger, work through the proof systems that solve it, write circuits in Circom, and finish by deploying a verifier on Fuji that checks a real proof on-chain. By the end, "the sender proves their balance covers the transfer without revealing it" should be a sentence you can implement rather than one you take on trust.

## What You Will Build

**Two artifacts you build and deploy yourself:**

- A **Circom circuit** that proves you know the preimage of a Poseidon hash, compiled to a Rank-1 Constraint System (R1CS) and proven with snarkjs.
- A **Groth16 verifier contract** deployed to Fuji Testnet that checks that proof for around 196,000 gas — including deliberately breaking it, so you can recognise the two silent failure modes on sight.

**One reference implementation you read and dissect:**

- A **private token contract** that stores encrypted balances, verifies transfer proofs, and updates balances homomorphically without decrypting them. You will not deploy this one: its transfer circuit is a substantial piece of cryptographic engineering in its own right, and shipping a half-audited version of it would teach the wrong lesson. We walk through it line by line, name every simplification against production eERC, and point you at the real source.

## A Note on the Acronyms

The outline below names several proof systems before the course defines them. You are not expected to know these yet — each gets a full lesson — but here is enough to read the contents page:

- **SNARK** — Succinct Non-interactive ARgument of Knowledge. A short proof, checkable quickly, with no back-and-forth between prover and verifier. **Groth16** and **PLONK** are both SNARKs.
- **STARK** — Scalable Transparent ARgument of Knowledge. Same goal, built on hashes instead of elliptic curves, needing no trusted setup but producing much larger proofs.
- **FHE** — Fully Homomorphic Encryption. Computing directly on encrypted data. Related to ZK, and covered in the final lesson.
- **MPC** — Multi-Party Computation. Several parties jointly computing a result without revealing their individual inputs.

## Course Content

### 1. Why ZK Matters for Privacy on Avalanche
- **The Privacy Problem**: What a transparent ledger leaks, and why the common privacy workarounds don't hold up.
- **ZK as the Answer**: Statement, witness, prover, verifier, and the three properties a proof system must satisfy.
- **ZK in eERC Context**: Where proofs appear in the eERC architecture, traced through a transfer.

### 2. ZK Proof Systems
- **SNARKs vs STARKs**: Trusted setup, proof size, verification cost, and quantum resistance.
- **Groth16 Deep Dive**: Three group elements, one pairing check, and the ceremony that keeps it safe.
- **PLONK and Beyond**: Universal setup, polynomial commitments, and the systems built on them.
- **Choosing Your Proof System**: How to pick one based on what your application pays for.

### 3. Circuits and Cryptographic Primitives
- **What Is a Circuit?** Arithmetic circuits, R1CS, and the cost of addition versus multiplication.
- **Writing Your First Circom Circuit**: A complete pipeline you can run, from `circom` to a verified proof.
- **Merkle Trees in ZK**: Set membership without revealing the member, and the constraint that makes it sound.
- **Pedersen Commitments and Homomorphic Encryption**: How a contract updates a balance it can't read.
- **Hash Functions in ZK**: Why SHA-256 costs far more than Poseidon inside a circuit.

### 4. Building ZK on Avalanche
- **ZK Verifier Contracts on C-Chain**: Compile, set up, export, deploy, and verify.
- **Integrating ZK with eERC Tokens**: Building a private token contract, and the mistakes to avoid.
- **Gas Optimization for ZK on Avalanche**: Aggregation, batch verification, and calldata costs.
- **Testing and Auditing ZK Contracts**: Under-constrained circuits and how to catch them.

### 5. Advanced Patterns and What's Next
- **ZK and Interchain Messaging**: Moving private state between Avalanche chains over ICM.
- **ZK on Custom L1s**: Precompiles that make Poseidon and BabyJubJub cheap.
- **Recursive Proofs and Proof Aggregation**: Collapsing many verifications into one.
- **The Road Ahead**: FHE, MPC, hardware provers, and where they meet ZK.

## Prerequisites

Before starting this course, you should have:

### The eERC Course
Finish [eERC Token Standard](/academy/blockchain/encrypted-erc) first. We assume you already know what an encrypted balance is and why anyone wants one.

### Solidity and the EVM
You should be comfortable writing, deploying, and calling a contract on Fuji without following a tutorial.

### Command Line Tools
Module 3 onward involves `npm`, a compiler binary, and a handful of CLI tools.

### Math
Algebra is enough. Modular arithmetic and finite fields come up constantly, but both are introduced when they first appear. There's no linear algebra or calculus anywhere in this course.

<Callout type="info">
No cryptography background is assumed. Elliptic curves, pairings, and commitment schemes are all introduced from scratch, at the level of detail you need to build with them.
</Callout>

## Learning Outcomes

By the end of this course, you will be able to:

- Explain what a zero-knowledge proof guarantees, and what it does not.
- Choose between Groth16, PLONK, and STARKs for a given application, and justify the choice on cost.
- Read a Circom circuit and spot an under-constrained signal.
- Compile a circuit, run a trusted setup, and deploy the generated verifier to Avalanche.
- Describe how ElGamal's homomorphic property lets a contract update a balance it can't decrypt.
- Estimate the gas cost of a ZK operation on C-Chain, and know which optimizations actually move that number.
- Evaluate whether recursion, aggregation, or a custom L1 precompile fits a given scaling problem.
